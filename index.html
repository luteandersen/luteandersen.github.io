Books

Philosophy · Essay

God Is Dead. Now What?

The Meaning of Life, the Individual, and Humanity

What remains when God is no longer the central explanation for life, morality and human purpose? This essay begins with that absence and refuses to replace it with another comforting mystery. Instead, it looks at what biology, evolution and consciousness can tell us about where our goals come from and why meaning still matters to creatures capable of questioning their own existence.

The argument moves from the origin of life to feeling, reward systems, metacognition, the relationship between the individual and society, and the possible direction of humanity as a whole. It asks whether a human purpose can be grounded in the kind of beings we actually are rather than handed down from outside the world.

It is an ambitious book, but not an academic obstacle course. The complete text is available free to read, so you can follow the argument, disagree with it and decide for yourself what—if anything—comes after the death of God.

Science Fiction · Absurdity

Jovian Chronicles, Vol. 1

Science Fiction, Cosmic Absurdity, and Other Human Disasters

A collection of stories about giant planets, failed missions, impossible machines, scientific absurdity and people who remain stubbornly human even when reality stops cooperating. A cosmic signal may turn out to be something embarrassingly ordinary; a technical innovation may work perfectly while ruining everything around it; an entire civilisation may be less prepared for reality than it thinks.

The scale moves freely between the Jovian system and familiar earthly disasters. There are astronauts, bureaucrats, engineers, lawyers, labourers, artificial minds and creatures whose existence raises questions nobody responsible thought to ask. The universe is vast, but its problems often come down to vanity, incompetence, paperwork and bad decisions.

Some stories are funny, others are bleak, and several are both at once. They have no required moral and make no promise to stay inside a single genre. The complete volume is free to read; enter wherever you like and leave with whatever interpretation survives the trip.
